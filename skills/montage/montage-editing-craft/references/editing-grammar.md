# Editing grammar and pacing

## Cut plan format

Keep it in the project's planning folder; it is also the bridge argument.

| # | Clip (exact name) | In | Out | Track | Purpose | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | A001_C0012.MP4 | 00:00:03:12 | 00:00:05:20 | V1 | establishing booth | speed 100% |
| 2 | interview_A.mov | 00:01:10:05 | 00:01:16:18 | V1/A1 | sentence "On accompagne…" | J-cut: audio 8 f early |

Durations come from in/out, not from guesses; record the source frame rate.

## Structures that work

**Ads (6 / 15 / 30 s)**

- 6 s: one idea — hook visual, brand, single line.
- 15 s: hook (0–3 s) → product/benefit (3–11 s) → brand + call to action (11–15 s).
- 30 s: hook → problem → solution → proof → call to action.

**Testimonial (60–90 s)**: who they are (5–10 s) → their situation → turning
point → result with specifics → recommendation line → brand.

**Event recap (30–90 s)**: arrival/energy → people and moments → key speaker
lines → crowd reaction → brand/date of next edition. Music-driven; speech bites
cut on sentences.

**LED loop (15–30 s, silent)**: brand → message 1 → message 2 → offer → brand;
holds set by reading time; every frame self-explanatory.

## Pacing by format (typical shot lengths)

| Format | Typical shot | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Social hype / recap | 0.5–2 s | variety of scale and motion; captions carry meaning |
| Corporate / testimonial | 3–6 s | let faces breathe; B-roll 2–4 s |
| Explainer with VO | 2–5 s per visual beat | cut with the sentence, not the word |
| LED loop | 2.5–6 s per message | reading time rules; no fast cutting under text |

Vary rhythm: three similar lengths in a row feel mechanical; a long shot after
quick cuts lands emphasis.

## Cut types and when to use them

- **Cut on action**: movement continues across the cut; hides the edit.
- **Match cut**: shape, colour or motion links two shots; good for transitions
  between ideas.
- **J-cut** (audio leads) and **L-cut** (audio lingers): 4–12 frames make
  dialogue and scene changes feel natural.
- **Jump cut**: acceptable in vlog/social styles; disguise in corporate work
  with B-roll or a scale change (≥ 20% punch-in).
- **Cutaway/B-roll**: covers edits in speech, shows what is said; keep it
  relevant to the words.
- **Smash cut**: abrupt contrast for humour or impact; sparingly.

## Speech editing

- Cut on sentence or phrase boundaries from a transcript; snap to real pauses.
- Remove breaths and fillers only when it stays natural; leave room tone under
  every edit (no dead silence).
- Check lip sync at every cut on camera; offset audio by frames if needed.
- Never alter meaning (no "frankenbites" that create new statements).

## Music editing

- Choose music for the edit's energy curve, then cut picture to the bars at
  key moments.
- Edit music on bar lines, hide edits under transients or hits, use the track's
  real ending.
- Duck under speech (6–12 dB) rather than lowering the whole track.
- Licence first; see sound-design-sfx.

## Transitions ladder

1. Hard cut (default; most seams want nothing else).
2. Native dissolve or dip, by exact transition name, for time passage.
3. A designed transition (.mogrt or AE precomp) matching the brand motion.
4. Generated first/last-frame bridges: hero seams only, credits authorised.

## Colour

- Normalise first (exposure, white balance, contrast) per shot, then match
  shots in a scene, then apply a look on an adjustment layer.
- Skin tones on the vectorscope skin line; avoid crushed blacks for web.
- LED: moderate contrast; avoid very bright large white areas; check on the
  device.
- Broadcast: legal levels per the broadcaster's sheet.
